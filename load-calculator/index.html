2D Load Calculator

Draw a structure, load it, and watch which parts are safe and which fail.
  1. Draw members — pick Member, click a start point, click an end point. Keep clicking to chain members (great for rafters and trusses). Press Esc or right-click to stop. Points snap to the 1 ft grid, to joints and onto other members.
  2. Add supports — pick Support and click where the structure sits on a wall, post or foundation. Pin holds in place, Roller only holds up/down, Fixed also prevents rotation (like a post set in concrete).
  3. Add loads — Point load for a concentrated weight at one spot; Spread load for weight along a member, in pounds per foot (e.g. roof snow + shingles, floor loads).
  4. Reshape freely — supports, point loads and members that end part-way along another member ride on it (shown as blue rings) instead of cutting it. Drag a member's end and they slide along with it; drag a ring to slide it along the member.
  5. Doubled / sistered boards — draw a new member right on top of an existing one (or part of it), or select a member and tap + Sister another board. The boards act together.
  6. Save & take it with you — 💾 My designs keeps named designs on this device. 📱 Send to phone shows a QR code: scan it with your phone's camera and the design opens and is saved there too.
  7. Read the colors — green is comfortable, yellow is getting there, red is at the limit, and dark red pulsing means it fails. Click any member for detailed numbers.
  8. Drag the Load scale slider or hit Find breaking point to see how much more load it takes before something gives.
Tips: Select tool: drag joints to reshape, drag empty space to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. Hold Shift to place without grid snapping. Change sizes and connections for a member in the right panel. “Pinned” connections act like a nailed/bolted truss joint that can rotate; “Rigid” joints are continuous.
Heads up: results use simplified allowable-stress checks (bending + push/pull, shear, and column buckling) with typical published values for Douglas Fir/SPF No.2 lumber, LVL, A992/A500 steel and 6061 aluminum. Use them for learning and rough sizing only — real designs need code load combinations, connection design and a qualified professional.
